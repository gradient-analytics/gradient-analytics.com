Data Science & Modeling

Applied data science, modeling, and AI for complex real-world systems.

Gradient partners with organizations of all sizes—from small teams to large enterprises—to design and deploy end-to-end analytical systems. We work across agriculture, biotech, and environmental domains, translating complex data into models and tools that support decisions, products, and strategy.

Our work spans the full pipeline: data ingestion, statistical and machine-learning models, process-based and spatial simulations, and clear communication of results—scaled to the problem at hand, not the size of the organization.

Core capabilities

Field & Vegetation Analytics

Design and analysis of field, greenhouse, and common garden trials, focused on plant performance, physiological response, and vegetation dynamics across treatments and environments.

Microbial & Soil Systems

Interpretation of soil chemistry and microbiome data, including DNA-based outputs from commercial platforms, connecting belowground signals to vegetation and system-level outcomes.

Climate & Geospatial Modeling

Integration of weather records, climate projections, and spatial data to model pest survival, disease risk, and ecosystem responses from local plots to continental scales.

Decision & Publication Support

End-to-end analytical support, from exploratory modeling to technical reports and manuscript-quality figures that inform decisions and withstand external scrutiny.

From messy data to clear decisions

Gradient specializes in real-world data: incomplete, multi-scale, and collected across sites, seasons, and systems. We design analytical workflows that integrate these pieces into coherent models rather than isolated analyses.

The emphasis is on effect sizes, uncertainty, and mechanisms—so results are actionable for management, product development, and long-term planning.

From soil DNA to global forecasts

Our analyses routinely span orders of magnitude in scale, from soil DNA and plot-level trials to forest stands, regions, and global climate scenarios.

This multi-scale perspective allows us to connect fine-grained biological signals to landscape-level patterns and future risk projections.
